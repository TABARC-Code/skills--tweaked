# Tension Taxonomy

Tensions are the places where forces in a system pull against each other. They are the most productive places to look - they reveal what the system is actually doing rather than what it claims to be doing.

Tensions do not need to be resolved to be useful. Naming them accurately changes the analysis.

---

## Structural Tensions

Arise from the design of the system itself. Often invisible because they're treated as normal.

**Role conflict**
Two roles have overlapping responsibilities or contradictory mandates. Common in matrixed organisations, joint projects, and anywhere authority has been distributed without being clarified.
*Diagnostic sign: two people both think something is their call, or both think it's someone else's.*

**Incentive misalignment**
The reward structure punishes the behaviour you want. A team incentivised on speed will cut corners on quality. A manager incentivised on headcount will resist efficiency. Follow the incentive; find the actual behaviour.
*Diagnostic sign: people behave sensibly from their own position but badly from the system's perspective.*

**Capacity overload**
A single person, team, or process is carrying more than it can reliably bear.
*Diagnostic sign: delays that appear random but cluster around one node; quality inconsistency; defensive or short communication from the overloaded party.*

**Governance gap**
Something needs to be decided but it's unclear who has the authority to decide it. Nothing moves until someone claims the authority or forces a resolution.
*Diagnostic sign: decisions keep getting escalated, deferred, or made by default.*

**Scale mismatch**
A process, tool, or relationship was designed for a different scale than it's now operating at. It worked fine before; it's failing now without anyone having changed it.
*Diagnostic sign: "it always used to work" - and nobody has touched it.*

---

## Relational Tensions

Arise between actors. Often the most politically sensitive to name.

**Trust deficit**
Two parties need each other to function but neither trusts the other enough to be fully honest.
*Diagnostic sign: over-documentation, cc-culture, parallel workstreams, hedging language in every message, decisions made with witnesses.*

**Status competition**
Actors are competing for recognition, authority, or credit in ways that distort their substantive decisions. Common in environments where symbolic victories substitute for real outcomes.
*Diagnostic sign: arguments that are technically about the work but feel personal; disproportionate resistance to ideas originating from certain people.*

**Historical injury**
Something that happened before the current situation is still shaping behaviour. Nobody refers to it directly. The effect is visible in over-sensitivity, disproportionate reactions, and unusual risk aversion from one party.
*Diagnostic sign: a reaction that doesn't fit the current situation - look for the earlier situation it actually fits.*

**Communication asymmetry**
One actor has information the other needs but hasn't shared. This may be deliberate (strategic withholding), incidental (nobody thought to mention it), or structural (different information streams reaching different people).
*Diagnostic sign: decisions that look irrational from one vantage point but make sense from another.*

**Loyalty split**
An actor has obligations to more than one party whose interests are in conflict. They cannot satisfy both and are managing the gap through ambiguity or delay.
*Diagnostic sign: inconsistent positions, unusual vagueness, delayed responses when the conflict is active.*

---

## Temporal Tensions

Arise from timing. Often manufactured or inherited rather than real.

**Urgency vs. quality**
Time pressure is forcing premature decisions.
*First question: who benefits from this feeling urgent? Urgency is often a negotiating tool.*

**Short vs. long-term conflict**
The move that solves the immediate problem makes the underlying problem worse. Common in debt-driven systems - technical, financial, relational.
*Diagnostic sign: the same problem keeps returning in slightly different forms.*

**Sequence lock**
Something needs to happen before something else can, and the prerequisite is blocked or delayed. Everything downstream waits - often without knowing why.
*Diagnostic sign: multiple teams feeling blocked by different things that all trace back to one unresolved upstream issue.*

**Irreversibility pressure**
A decision point is approaching after which the options narrow significantly. The pressure of this tends to produce either paralysis or recklessness.
*Diagnostic sign: unusual anxiety about a deadline; or unusual recklessness that seems out of character.*

**Premature closure**
A decision was made earlier that is now constraining the current situation - but is being treated as fixed when it could in principle be revisited.
*Diagnostic sign: "we decided that already" being used to close down exploration.*

---

## Informational Tensions

Arise from knowledge distribution.

**Missing information**
A key fact is absent. Decisions are being made on incomplete data, often without decision-makers knowing what they don't know.
*Diagnostic sign: confident plans with a conspicuous gap where evidence should be.*

**Conflicting information**
Two apparently reliable sources say contradictory things. Usually a sign that sources are measuring different things, or that one has an interest in a particular answer.
*Diagnostic sign: the conflict is treated as a contradiction to resolve rather than a signal to investigate.*

**Unspoken knowledge**
Everyone in the room knows something nobody will say. Common around performance problems, organisational dysfunction, or uncomfortable truths about a product or decision.
*Diagnostic sign: notable absence of the obvious question; careful language; unanimous but vague agreement.*

**Framing capture**
The problem has been defined in a way that makes certain solutions invisible. The frame is not examined because it's presented as neutral.
*Diagnostic sign: the solution space is suspiciously narrow; alternatives that seem obvious from outside aren't being considered inside.*

**Confidence miscalibration**
People are more certain than the evidence warrants - or less certain than they should be, depending on their position. Both distort decisions.
*Diagnostic sign: certainty that isn't backed by evidence; or paralysis in the face of adequate-but-imperfect information.*

---

## Using This Taxonomy

Tensions often cluster. A trust deficit usually accompanies communication asymmetry. Incentive misalignment produces status competition. Sequence lock generates urgency vs. quality pressure.

When multiple tensions are present, identify the primary one - the tension that, if it resolved, would most reduce the others. That is often the most productive place to intervene.
